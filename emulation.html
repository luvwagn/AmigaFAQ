Emulation

Running Amiga software on modern computers and devices.

Can I run Amiga software on a modern computer?

Yes. Amiga emulators recreate the hardware in software, accurately enough to run nearly all games, demos and applications. You will need an emulator, a Kickstart ROM (or the free AROS replacement) and the software you want to run, usually as ADF or WHDLoad files.

Which emulator should I use?

WinUAE (Windows) is the most accurate and complete Amiga emulator and the reference that others are measured against. FS-UAE runs on Windows, macOS and Linux. Amiberry is designed for Linux devices such as the Raspberry Pi. vAmiga is a macOS emulator focused on accuracy for the A500, A1000 and A2000.

Amiga emulation is also available inside RetroArch through a libretro core.

Where do I get a Kickstart ROM for an emulator?

The easiest legal route is a licensed package such as Amiga Forever, which includes ROMs and Workbench. If you own a real Amiga, you can also copy its ROM yourself with a transfer tool. AROS offers a free replacement ROM, which works well for many programs but is less compatible with games than the original.

What is the A500 Mini?

The A500 Mini is a small plug-and-play replica of the Amiga 500 released in 2022 by Retro Games Ltd. It connects by HDMI, comes with a mouse and gamepad and a selection of built-in games, and can load additional games from a USB stick. It runs on emulation rather than original Amiga hardware.

Can an emulator help me with a real Amiga?

Yes. Many owners use an emulator such as WinUAE to set up and install software onto a CompactFlash or SD card, then move the card into their real Amiga. Emulators are also handy for testing a game or setup before committing to it on original hardware.
