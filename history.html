History

Where the Amiga came from and what happened to it.

Who created the Amiga?

The Amiga began at a small California company founded in 1982, originally called Hi-Toro and soon renamed Amiga Corporation. Its chipset was designed by a team led by Jay Miner, who had earlier worked on Atari's home computer chips. The prototype was code-named "Lorraine".

Top

What does the name Amiga mean?

"Amiga" is Spanish for a female friend. The founders wanted a friendly name, and it also conveniently appeared ahead of Apple and Atari in alphabetical listings.

Top

How did Commodore end up owning the Amiga?

Amiga Corporation ran short of money while developing its chipset and took funding from Atari, which came with strings attached. In 1984 Commodore stepped in and bought Amiga Corporation outright, leading to a legal dispute with Atari. Commodore launched the first model, the Amiga 1000, in 1985.

Top

How was the Amiga launched?

The Amiga 1000 was unveiled in July 1985 at a gala event at Lincoln Center in New York. In one of the best-remembered moments, artist Andy Warhol created a digital portrait of singer Debbie Harry live on stage using the new computer.

Read more: Warhol & The Computer (Computer History Museum).

Top

What was the Amiga used for?

At home, especially in Europe, the Amiga was one of the leading games machines of the late 1980s and early 1990s, with studios such as Psygnosis, DMA Design, Team17 and Sensible Software making landmark titles. It also became a creative tool: NewTek's Video Toaster made the Amiga 2000 a low-cost video production system used in television, including for visual effects in early Babylon 5. The Amiga was also central to the demoscene and to tracker music.

Top

What happened to Commodore?

Commodore struggled in the early 1990s as PC and console competition grew, and it filed for bankruptcy in 1994. The Amiga rights then passed through several owners, starting with the German company Escom, which itself went bankrupt in 1996, and later Gateway.

Development of the operating system continued through other companies, and the community has kept the platform alive with new hardware, software and updated versions of AmigaOS.

Further reading: what are some good books on Amiga history?

These books cover the Amiga's story from different angles:

  • The Future Was Here: The Commodore Amiga by Jimmy Maher (MIT Press, 2012) - a thoughtful history of the hardware design, the culture around the machine, and why it mattered. Maher also covers the Amiga in his long-running blog, The Digital Antiquarian.
  • Commodore: The Amiga Years by Brian Bagnall - a detailed, interview-driven account of the people and company politics behind the Amiga at Commodore.
  • Commodore Amiga: a visual Commpendium (Bitmap Books) - a large, beautifully designed art book of Amiga games and graphics.
  • The Story of the Commodore Amiga in Pixels by Chris Wilkins - a game-focused history built around developer interviews and screenshots.
  • Freax: The Brief History of the Computer Demoscene by Tamas Polgar - the story of the demoscene, in which the Amiga played a central part.
